Strength first. Then we stand together.

We do not need millions of followers. We need Maharashtrians strong in body, earnings and learning, families that back their children, and communities so well organised that no one, however powerful, dares to wrong them. That is the path Shivaji Maharaj showed.

// Who we are

We are not a people who wait to be rescued.

For centuries this land has given India its saints and reformers, its courage in public life, and a working example of people of many faiths living and building side by side. We are not asking for pity. We are asking our own people to grow strong again, and to stand together.

// The path of Shivaji Maharaj

Strengthen ourselves first.

He built strength before he needed it: in his people, his forts, his navy and his treasury. We begin the same way, with four strengths.

Health

Physical strength first. Daily training, sport, kusti, mallakhamb, and the fort treks that built our ancestors.

Wealth

Earn, save and own. Start something, however small. Money in our own hands is freedom in our own hands.

Education

Skill over certificates. Learn with your hands and your head, from the trades to engineering, medicine, the IITs and IIMs.

Family and community

Families that back their children, and communities that turn up when one of their own is wronged.

// What we face

Twelve issues. Twelve answers.

Each one comes with what is happening, what we do about it, and one move you can make this week.

01

The exodus

What is happening

Young people leave their villages and districts because the work is not there. Homes empty out, and parents grow old alone.

What we do

Bring work home. District circles, local ventures, remote jobs, and a welcome for those who come back.

→ Write down three businesses your taluka needs and nobody runs yet.

02

Skills that do not meet work

What is happening

Degrees without readiness. Too many of us were taught to wait for the right job, and too few to do willing work with our hands.

What we do

Skill before certificate. Apprenticeships, mentor hours and the Academy's open pathways, from trades to IIT and medicine.

→ Pick one skill and give it thirty days.

03

Our farmers

What is happening

About one in every two farmer suicides in India happens in Maharashtra, most in rain-fed Vidarbha and Marathwada, where irrigation is thin and debt runs deep.

What we do

Farmers who own the chain: producer companies, storage, processing, direct markets and fair credit, so no single trader or lender holds a village.

→ Connect one farmer you know to a producer company or a direct buyer.

04

Dignity, not pity

What is happening

Our farmers, students and schools are shown as charity cases, in photographs used to raise money elsewhere.

What we do

We fix our own schools and roads, and hold those who govern to account for the rest. No begging, no pity, no poverty pictures.

→ Give one hour to one school near you, as a skilled volunteer.

05

Schools, fees and Marathi

What is happening

Some schools skip Marathi or price ordinary families out with fee hikes.

What we do

Use the law. Marathi is compulsory from Class 1 to 10 in every school, on every board. Fee hikes must pass the Parent-Teacher Association's executive committee.

→ Ask your child's school, in writing, for its Marathi timetable and its PTA committee's minutes.

06

Land, forests and sanctuaries

What is happening

Farmland, village commons, forests and sanctuaries are lost to encroachment and to transfers that do not stand up to scrutiny.

What we do

A land watch in every district: records checked, evidence gathered, complaints filed with the Collector, forest department or court.

→ Download your family's 7/12 extract and check it is correct.

07

Our streets

What is happening

Road rage, harassment and crime, and a crowd that films and walks away.

What we do

We do not look away. Witness, call 112, help the injured. The law protects anyone who helps a road accident victim in good faith.

→ Save 112, and learn basic first aid this month.

08

Divided by pressure

What is happening

Religion, caste, party and money pull us apart, while the work that would lift us all waits.

What we do

One rule: caste, faith, region and party stay outside the work. We build together or not at all.

→ Build something with someone outside your usual circle.

09

Afraid to start

What is happening

We hesitate to open a stall, a workshop or a service business, and leave whole trades to others.

What we do

No work is small. A roadside stall is a business, and a business is a school. Where trade sits in a few hands, we compete and cooperate.

→ Start a weekend business, and buy from the builders near you.

10

Language and culture at home

What is happening

Children grow up without Marathi books, songs, festivals or clothes of their own.

What we do

We never force our language on anyone. We keep it alive in our own homes: we speak it, read it, sing it, dress in it and celebrate in it.

→ Read one Marathi book a month with your child.

11

His forts

What is happening

Forts are littered, encroached on and treated as picnic spots.

What we do

His forts deserve the reverence we give a temple. We demand protection: no liquor, no litter, no encroachment, and money for conservation.

→ Become a Fort Steward for one fort.

12

A government that acts for us first

What is happening

Maharashtra aims to become a trillion-dollar economy. That only counts if it reaches the farmer, the student and the small trader.

What we do

Organised, lawful pressure: RTI, gram sabhas, ward meetings and public hearings, with evidence in hand and numbers behind it.

→ Attend your next gram sabha or ward meeting, and take one question.

On farmers: if you or someone you know is struggling, Tele-MANAS answers free on 14416, around the clock.

// Ganimi kava for citizens

Strategy, not anger.

His method won against larger armies by knowing the ground and moving fast. Ours wins against indifference the same way, inside the law.

01

Know the ground

Know the laws, offices, records and people of your district better than anyone.

02

Move in small teams

Five people with evidence achieve more than five thousand with hashtags.

03

Be prepared before it matters

Records gathered, complaints drafted and numbers ready, before anyone expects you.

04

Strike at the wrong, not the person

We target the illegal act, the missing road, the unlawful fee. Never a person's life, home or community.

05

Never harm the innocent

No violence, no threats, no harassment. Strength that has to shout is not strength.

// To our parents

You kept us safe. Now let us dare.

You worked so that we would never go without. You chose the safe path for us because you had seen what the unsafe one cost.

We are grateful. But a people that never takes a risk never builds anything of its own. Stop arguing over which secure job is best. Ask your child what they want to try, and help them try it properly: a plan, a mentor, a limit on the money, and your blessing.

If they fail, let them fail at home, where you can stand behind them. That is how strong families are made.

// To the young

Unite to build, not to trend.

A social media campaign lasts a day. A district circle lasts a decade.

Meet in person. Learn the laws that touch your village or ward. Turn up to gram sabhas, ward meetings and public hearings with evidence and with numbers. Make sure the government of a state this rich acts first for its own people.

Train your body, sharpen your skill, earn your own money, and stand with each other. Nobody will dare to take a strong people lightly.

// Our conduct

How we keep this movement clean.

Strength without discipline turns into the very thing we oppose.

  1. We never harm, threaten or harass anyone.
  2. We never target a person for their language, faith, caste or where they come from. We target wrongs, with evidence.
  3. We never force Marathi on anyone. We keep it alive in our own homes, schools and festivals.
  4. We act through law, records, courts, elected bodies and our own numbers.
  5. We take no money and no side in any election.
