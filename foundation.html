Our story

One movement, and a single practice: connect, create, develop, contribute, improve, progress, and begin again.

The challenge

Maharashtra has talent, capital, institutions and a worldwide diaspora. They are rarely connected to the people who need them, at the moment they need them.

Our answer

A movement in which people find what works, vouch for those behind it, and help one another directly, around the Progress Loop.

Our resolve

That every Maharashtrian becomes a builder, and that the loop keeps turning, one person at a time.

// Those who came before

Movements that began with one person and outlived them.

We hold no link to these institutions. We study how they worked, and take one lesson from each.

Karmaveer Bhaurao Patil · Satara · 1919

Rayat Shikshan Sanstha

Education through self-help. Students earned their keep and their schooling together, and the society grew to more than seven hundred institutions.

→ Name the method, not the mission.

Source ↗

Sree Narayana Guru · Kerala · 1903

SNDP Yogam

A teaching compressed into a line people could carry, then turned into schools and a lasting mass organisation.

→ Keep the creed short enough to remember.

Source ↗

Swami Vivekananda · Bengal · 1897

Ramakrishna Mission

One motto joins the person and the world: one's own freedom, and the good of all.

→ Begin with yourself, end with others.

Source ↗

Rabindranath Tagore · Santiniketan · 1921

Visva-Bharati

A five-student school under open sky that became a university for the world.

→ Start small. Stay open.

Source ↗

Ifan ab Owen Edwards · Wales · 1922

Urdd Gobaith Cymru

One appeal in a children's magazine built a youth movement for the Welsh language in which millions have since taken part.

→ Go through the young.

Source ↗

Douglas Hyde · Ireland · 1893

Conradh na Gaeilge

A language movement that insisted it belonged to no church and no party. Its founder resigned when it took a side.

→ Write the neutrality line, and keep it.

// Our inspiration

Six values from how he built.

Each rests on a cited source. Popular accounts without a source are not used.

01

Protecting the livelihood

He ordered his army not to touch so much as a leaf of a cultivator's crop, and to pay for grain and fodder. (Letter from the Chiplun camp, 1673) Source

02

Appointment on merit

The Ashtapradhan council of 1674 was appointed, not inherited; it became hereditary only after his time. Source

03

Talent from every quarter

His navy drew officers and sailors of many backgrounds, chosen for capability alone. Source

04

Preparation before necessity

Sindhudurg sea fort was begun in 1664, in anticipation of threats from the sea. Source

05

Building in one's own language

He commissioned the Rajavyavaharakosha, a lexicon of administrative terms, so the state could govern in its own language. Source

06

Respect for women

Jadunath Sarkar notes that even Khafi Khan, a chronicler hostile to him, recorded his respect for women. Source

Claims we do not use

  • That "thirteen of his twenty-two generals were Muslim": no source has been found.
  • The Ajnapatra (1715) cited as his own words; it was written later, by Ramchandrapant Amatya.
  • That the dabbawalas are "Six Sigma certified": no body has ever certified them.

// Independence

The Civic Charter.

We want more Maharashtrians to understand government, take part, ask searching questions and, in time, serve. The Foundation itself takes no side.

Members are free to contest elections. They keep their profile, but may not campaign through the Foundation.

  1. The Foundation endorses no party, candidate or elected representative, at any level.
  2. Founders, Panel members and stewards hold no party office and contest no election while serving.
  3. No page, emblem or list of the Foundation may be used in any campaign.
  4. Every administration is assessed against the same standard. Conclusions follow the evidence.
  5. During an election in any area, no new comparative assessment of that area is published; only impartial voter education.

// Conduct

Open to all, by design.

Inclusion

  • No field for caste or religion, anywhere.
  • No circle organised by caste or religion.
  • Every resource, for every community, presented impartially.
  • Pride in Maharashtra requires no one to be diminished.

Standards of conduct

  1. Contribute with usefulness and courtesy; challenge ideas, not persons.
  2. No hostility towards any caste, religion, region or language.
  3. No party campaigning, commerce or solicitation of money.
  4. State facts with sources; mark opinion as opinion.
  5. Respect privacy: no one's number, address or image without consent.
