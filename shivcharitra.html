// The promise

Tell it from the sources, so no one can take it away.

A story that rests on evidence cannot be bent by any party, publisher or syllabus. That is the only way we will tell it.

// What we will make

Six kinds of work, for every age.

Digital copies free, always. Printed copies at cost, through schools, libraries and anyone willing to print and give them away.

Ages 5 to 8

Picture books

Short, beautiful, read aloud at home and in anganwadis. One fort, one story, one lesson per book.

Ages 9 to 12

Chapter books

His life in twelve short books, each ending with a page of sources a teacher can check.

Ages 12 and up

Comics and graphic novels

Drawn panel by panel from the chronicles, with a footnote wherever the story rests on a source.

Every age

Games

The Fort Quiz below, a print-and-play fort board game, and a council card game about choosing ministers on merit.

Families and schools

Fort trail passport

A stamp book for the twelve UNESCO forts and beyond, with a page on what happened at each.

Classrooms

Teacher's kit

Lesson plans, maps and primary-source extracts that fit an ordinary school period.

// Learnings from his life

Eight lessons, each tied to an event.

Not slogans. Each lesson points to something that happened, and to where you can read about it.

01

Begin young, begin small

Torna, 1646. He was sixteen, and the first fort was a modest one. Source

02

Choose the ground

Pratapgad, 1659. He drew a larger army into forest country that favoured his men. Source

03

Loyalty is earned

Pavankhind, 1660. Baji Prabhu Deshpande held the pass until the signal came from Vishalgad. Source

04

Build before you need it

Sindhudurg, 1664. A navy and sea forts, begun before the threat arrived. Source

05

Lose well, then rebuild

Purandar, 1665. A treaty that gave up twenty-three forts, followed by years of patient recovery. Source

06

Appoint on merit

Raigad, 1674. A council of eight ministers, chosen for their work. Source

07

Govern in your own language

The Rajavyavaharakosha, a lexicon so the state could run in its own words. Source

08

Protect the cultivator

His orders forbade soldiers to take a farmer's crop without paying for it. Source

// The standard

How every page will be checked.

We use

  • Contemporary records: the Sabhasad Bakhar, Parmanand's Shivabharat, and English, Dutch and Portuguese factory letters.
  • Historians who show their sources, and the corrections later historians made to them.
  • A footnote wherever a page rests on one source, and a note wherever sources disagree.

We do not use

  • Popular claims with no source behind them, however often they are repeated.
  • Any version of his life written to win votes or to wound a community.
  • Invented dialogue presented as history. Where a comic imagines a scene, it says so.

// The source shelf

Start where the historians start.

Old translations and studies, many now out of copyright in India and free to read. Later historians corrected parts of them, so we read them alongside newer work.

01

Siva Chhatrapati · Surendranath Sen, 1920

An English translation of the Sabhasad Bakhar, written by a courtier for Chhatrapati Rajaram.

↗02

Shivaji and His Times · Jadunath Sarkar, 1920

The classic English study. Its preface warns against the false legends already growing in his day.

↗03

Online literature on Shivaji Maharaj · Shivaji University

A compiled library of full texts in Marathi and English, kept by the university in Kolhapur.

↗04

Maratha Military Landscapes of India · UNESCO

The nomination dossier, maps and evaluation for the twelve inscribed forts.
